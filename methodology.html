Archive methodology

How the archive decides what belongs, which sources control, how dates are recorded and how superseded rules are preserved.

← Archive home · Researcher kit · Cite & reuse

Purpose

The archive answers a narrow historical question: what did an official Canadian immigration source say was the applicable rule or policy state on a given date? It is a research and reference archive, not legal advice and not a substitute for checking the current controlling government source for a live matter.

Controlling source hierarchy

  1. Legislation, regulations, Ministerial Instructions or other controlling legal instruments.
  2. Official Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada / Government of Canada program-delivery, eligibility, policy or notice pages.
  3. Official provincial or territorial immigration-program pages and notices.
  4. Official archived or superseded government material when needed to reconstruct a historical state.
Third-party material cannot become the controlling source. It may help discover a change, but an archive event is not accepted until the controlling evidence is official.

Date discipline

The archive keeps three concepts separate:

  • announcement_date — when an official announcement or notice was published, if known.
  • rule_observed_from — the earliest date on which the archive verified the stated rule from the cited official evidence.
  • effective_from — the legal or program effective date only when an official source explicitly supports it or the controlling instrument establishes it.

If a source announces a change but gives no separate effective date, effective_from remains null. Publication date is not silently converted into a coming-into-force date.

Preservation rule

History is never silently overwritten. When a later rule supersedes an archived rule, the older event remains available. Where the evidence supports it, predecessor and successor records are linked through supersedes_event_id and superseded_by_event_id, and effective_to is populated only when a defensible end date exists.

Official-source anomalies are preserved explicitly rather than normalized. The archive records what the official source actually said and explains the bounded historical interpretation.

Acceptance gate

A new event must have all of the following:

  1. a material rule, eligibility, evidence, filing, status/work-permit, processing-instruction or officer-guidance change;
  2. an official controlling source;
  3. a defensible date basis;
  4. a clear topic key;
  5. explicit transition or supersession treatment where applicable; and
  6. a verification date.

No entry is created from rumours, social posts, commentary, press coverage, unofficial summaries or an inferred effective date.

Coverage interpretation

A result means the archive contains a verified record for that topic/date. A missing result does not mean no rule existed; it means this release does not yet contain enough verified historical evidence to answer that date/topic safely.

Version 0.8.0 contains 36 selected material events. It is intentionally selective rather than a complete history of Canadian immigration law or policy.

Machine-readable contract

events.json is the canonical structured release. events.csv is the flat export. Field semantics are documented in the data dictionary and the permissive machine contract is published as event-schema.json.

Current official government sources control if they conflict with this archive.
